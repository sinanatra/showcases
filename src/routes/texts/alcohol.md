# Blood Alcohol Level

Alcohol lowers inhibitions and can therefore encourage criminal behaviour. Of 2.132 analyzed police reports from 2019–November 2025, 345 cases (16,2%) mention the consumption of alcohol. Overall the police documented 231 breathalyser tests ranging from 0.00‰ to 4.38‰, and an average of approximate 1.92‰. The specific crimes associated with these incidents correspond to the vast variety of xenophobic, antisemitic, nazi-related and queerphobic crimes documented throughout the entire dataset. Though some offences occur more frequently by actions of an alcoholized person than others. This short deep dive is looking at five usual crimes within the dataset by analyzing certain keywords in combination with alcohol.

Xenophobic insults and attacks are mentioned in 291 of all police reports, while 73 (25,1%) mention that the offender was alcoholized. Of the 168 antisemitic incidents, 21 cases (12,5%) mention the involvement of alcohol, and so do 23 (10%) of the 230 recorded queerphobic cases. 

A different picture emerges from following specific nazi-related claims and symbols. The typical vocal and gestic signals “Sieg Heil”, “Heil Hitler”, and the Hitler salute are mentioned in 268 of all cases, while 109 police reports (40,1%) note that the perpetrator was liquorized. On the other hand, only 5 of 521 (1%) of the reported cases mentioning swastikas also include alcohol. The latter can be explained by the fact that most swastika graffiti paintings are linked to unknown suspects. So, the estimated number of alcoholized offenders could be significantly higher. 

To summarize, certain crimes – and especially those related to racism and right-wing extremism – occur more often with the involvement of alcohol than the overall average of cases mentioning alcohol. This is also the case, due to the vast amount of unknown suspects in swastika painting crimes, which make up almost a quarter of all police reports within the dataset. The data does not assume that people are more likely to commit, when drinking alcohol, but it assumes that right-wing extremists are less inhibited to share nazi ideology.
